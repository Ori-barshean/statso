    אודות

    מה זה statso

    statso אוסף נתונים כלכליים ישראליים ממקורות רשמיים ומפרסם אותם כקבצים בכתובות קבועות, כדי שכלים אחרים — גיליונות אקסל, אתרים, מחשבונים — יוכלו למשוך מהם נתונים ישירות.

    הנתונים נאספים מהלשכה המרכזית לסטטיסטיקה (מדד המחירים לצרכן ומדד תשומות הבנייה למגורים) ומבנק ישראל (ריבית בנק ישראל, מועד ההחלטה הבאה ושערי החליפין היציגים של המטבעות).

    כלי החשבונאות והמיסוי כוללים דו״ח נסיעה לחו״ל: התקרות קבועות בקוד לפי טפסי הדיווח לשנות המס, ואינן נתונים שנאספים אוטומטית. שנת המס שנבחרה קובעת את התקרות. סכומי חינוך הילדים לפי תקנה 2(2)(ז) לתקנות מס הכנסה (ניכוי הוצאות מסויימות), התשל״ב־1972: הסכום ל־2025 מנוסח התקנה, הסכום ל־2026 מאתרי רואי חשבון ואינו ממקור רשמי, והסכומים ל־2018–2024 שוחזרו לפי מדד המחירים לצרכן בארה״ב ואינם מאומתים.

    הנתונים מתרעננים אוטומטית כל לילה ב־02:00 שעון ישראל. אם התוכן שנוצר זהה לקיים, לא נשמרת גרסה חדשה.

    What statso is

    statso collects Israeli economic data from official sources and publishes it as files at stable addresses, so that other tools — spreadsheets, websites, calculators — can pull the numbers directly.

    The data is collected from the Central Bureau of Statistics (the consumer price index and the residential construction inputs price index) and from the Bank of Israel (the Bank of Israel interest rate, the next decision date, and the representative exchange rates of the currencies).

    Accounting and tax tools include a Foreign Travel Report: caps are fixed in code from the reporting forms for each tax year, rather than collected automatically. The selected tax year determines the caps. The children’s education amounts follow regulation 2(2)(g) of the Income Tax (Deduction of Certain Expenses) Regulations, 5732-1972: the 2025 amount is from the text of the regulation, the 2026 amount comes from accountants’ websites and is not an official source, and the 2018–2024 amounts were reconstructed from the US consumer price index and are not verified.

    The data refreshes automatically every night at 02:00 Israel time. If the generated content is identical to what is already published, no new version is stored.

    מחשבון ההצמדה

    סכום מוצמד = הסכום × (מדד היעד ÷ מדד הבסיס).

    במצב ״מדד ידוע״ המדד הנלקח לחודש שנבחר הוא זה של החודש שקדם לו; במצב ״מדד בגין״ נלקח המדד של אותו חודש עצמו. המדריך מסביר את ההבחנה.

    Consumer price index

    The series is monthly and starts in 09/1951, with hundreds of observations at present.

    Two values are published for each month: the reading as the CBS published it, in the base in force at the time; and a value chained to a single base — September 1951 = 100.

    The CBS replaces the index base every few years, and there have been 22 different bases over the history of the series. The chaining here is not a product of conversion coefficients: the CBS itself publishes each month's value in every earlier base, and we read the September 1951 value directly. No rounding error accumulates over hundreds of months.

    Bank of Israel rate

    The source is a continuous daily series starting 27/01/1994.

    We publish change points only: one row for every date on which the rate actually moved. They are derived by walking the daily series and recording a row whenever the value differs from the previous day. There are dozens of change points at present.

    The linkage calculator

    Linked amount = amount × (target index ÷ base index).

    In “known index” mode the reading taken for the chosen month is the previous month's; in “index for the month” mode it is that month's own reading. The guide explains the distinction.

    Foreign Travel Report

    • One trip per report, for a company or self-employed business. Traveller count is the number of traveller rows (at least one). Personal details are not stored in the browser. The selected tax year sets the caps even when travel dates fall in another year.
    • Trip days include departure and return. Business days must be an integer from 1 to total days. Lodging nights equal business days; in partial per-diem mode, lodging days K determine all lodging caps and must be from 1 to business days minus 1.
    • Lodging: the first-7-night cap applies to the actual nights, up to 7, multiplied by travellers. For nights 8–90, the average per night per traveller is fully deductible up to the threshold; above it, 75% is deductible within the threshold and maximum. Over 90 nights, all nights from the first use that year's threshold, without the 75% rule.
    • The 28 listed countries receive a 25% uplift for lodging, per diem and car rental, including both the threshold and maximum for nights 8–90: F′=1.25F, M′=1.25M. The default is Other country, without an uplift. Gifts receive no uplift.
    • Per diem: entering any lodging amount applies the with-lodging rate to all business days; otherwise the without-lodging rate applies. Partial mode uses lodging days at the lower rate and remaining days at the higher rate, multiplied by travellers and the uplift. Car rental uses the daily cap, business days, travellers and uplift. Gifts: USD 15 per traveller.
    • Flights, insurance, public transport, telephone, hosting a foreign resident and other expenses are fully deductible against receipts. First-class fares are limited to the business-class fare, but the tool does not enforce this.
    • Children’s education: under regulation 2(2)(g) of the Income Tax (Deduction of Certain Expenses) Regulations, 5732-1972, on a continuous stay abroad of over 10 months, education expenses are recognised for children who have not yet turned 18 in the tax year, up to an amount per child per month. A trip is over 10 months when trip days × 12 ÷ 365 is greater than 10, that is, at least 305 days; on a shorter trip the row is not offered. The cap is the number of children × the number of months (trip days × 12 ÷ 365, prorated by days) × the amount per child per month in dollars, with no 25% uplift. The amount for 2025 is USD 890, as in the regulation; for 2026 it is USD 914 from accountants’ websites, which are not an official source; the amounts for 2018–2024 were reconstructed from 890 by the US consumer price index (November to November, as regulation 2(2)(f) sets), rounded to the dollar, and not verified; for 2027 and later the 2026 amount is used until another is given. The Commissioner may allow an amount above this; the tool limits the cap to the amount in the regulation.
    • The “Lodging — nights 8–90” row is offered only from 8 business days; an amount already entered in it stays visible and is included in the calculation even when the days fall below 8.
    • Over 90 nights the lodging is entered in a single row, “Lodging — all nights (over 90 nights)”; the other two lodging rows are not offered. An amount entered in a row that was hidden is kept but is not included in the calculation, and returning to a shorter trip brings it back.
    • Beside every expense field that has a cap, the allowed cap is shown and updated with every change in the tax year, destination, dates, days, lodging, travellers and the field’s currency. Under it is the same calculation as in the explanation column of the table. Before the dates are entered only the rate in dollars is shown.
    • Expenses and USD caps are converted to ILS before comparison, using the latest rate published on or before departure. JPY quotes are divided by 100. When no rate falls within 7 days, a warning names the actual date and the last published rate is used. Dates before the data begins block calculation.
    • No intermediate rounding: rows retain full precision and display two decimal places. Each total is rounded once to two decimal places, half up. Deductible expense is the lesser of the expense and cap; the adjustment is the positive difference.
    • Narrative forms take precedence over Excel templates unless a specific decision states otherwise. Derived-cap notes are shown in 2018, 2020, 2021, 2024 and 2025, including in exports.
    • 2018: USD 208.5 for nights 8–90 is derived as 75% of USD 278, instead of USD 200 in the form. The country list comes from 2019 because 2018 has none.
    • 2020: USD 216.75 for nights 8–90 is derived as 75% of USD 289; no narrative form is available.
    • 2021: caps come from the explanatory text in the Excel template; no narrative form is available.
    • 2024: USD 259.5 for nights 8–90 is derived as 75% of USD 346, instead of USD 346 in the form.
    • 2025: the ordinary-country first-7-night cap of USD 355 is derived from USD 443.75 in the form, which includes the 25% uplift.

    Accommodations actually implemented

    Navigation, focus and structure

    • a "skip to main content" button at the start of every page
    • moving focus to the new page's heading on every page transition, so screen readers announce it
    • Each guide has its own address: for a guide opened from the guide list, the browser's Back button closes it, returns to the guide list and moves focus to the page heading (a guide opened from a direct link has no earlier guide-list entry in the history, so Back goes to whatever was open before the link)
    • "Tools" and "Accounting & Tax Tools" navigation menus that are fully keyboard-operable, including closing with the Escape key
    • each of the "Tools" and "Accounting & Tax Tools" submenus in the navigation, when it is open only because the mouse rests on it, closes with the Escape key without moving the mouse or the keyboard focus, and opens again when the mouse leaves the menu area and comes back
    • focus returning to the language button after a language is chosen or the menu is closed with the Escape key
    • a prominent keyboard-focus indicator on every interactive element that is never hidden under the sticky header, including when it grows taller on narrow screens, and is not cut off at the edge of the scroll areas of the tables, illustrations and code blocks
    • a non-color indicator (a bottom bar) for the current page in navigation, also in forced colors mode such as Windows High Contrast
    • in forced colors mode, the action buttons that are a plain color fill with no border in the normal view — sending the contact form, copying the code, the "Copy" and "Switch to Power Query" buttons in the guides and the skip-to-content button — are drawn with a border, so they do not look like ordinary text
    • a distinct browser-tab title for every page
    • Section headings on the information pages (about, terms, privacy and this statement), real lists where the text enumerates items (the privacy policy and this statement), and a heading on each of the three data cards on the home page
    • A name for every year-and-month pair (base and target month in the home-page calculator and the index-linkage tool, start and end month in the historical index data), so a screen reader can tell them apart
    • The decorative logo mark, and the logo card among the data cards on the home page, are hidden from screen readers, so the home link's name matches its visible text, and the two links to the CBS interface on the method page have distinct visible text
    • keyboard-focusable, keyboard-scrollable containers for the historical data tables, the exchange-rate table, guide illustrations, and sample-code blocks
    • Sample-code blocks and guide illustrations are marked as named regions, and the code itself is marked as English text
    • Every illustration in the guides has a description in words that a screen reader reads (for example: an Excel illustration of the Data tab with the Get Data button highlighted), instead of an internal name for the picture
    • The "Copy" buttons in the guides are told apart by their accessible names, according to the file each one copies, and the arrow in the "Back" and "All tools" links is hidden from screen readers, so the link's name is its visible text
    • The symbols on the cards on the tools page, the ✓ in the language menu and the ✓ in the copy confirmation are decoration and are not read as part of the component's name
    • English terms and phrases inside the Hebrew text of the method, privacy policy and terms of use pages, of this statement and of the steps of the Excel import guide are marked as English text (except for the site's name)
    • An address inside the site that does not lead to an existing page (the part of the address after the # sign, for example a tool or a guide that does not exist) shows a "Page not found" view with a main heading, its own browser-tab title, focus moved to the heading, and links to the home page, the calculation tools and the guides, instead of silently showing the home page or the guide list
    • An address that does not exist on the site's server shows a standalone error page (404) in Hebrew and English, with a single main heading, a link to the site's home page and a visible focus indicator
    • A "Site map" page, linked from the footer of every page, gathers links to the home page, the calculation tools, the guides, the export code and the information pages, as another way to reach them besides the menu

    • click targets at least 24 pixels tall

    • the chart's axes, legend and event lines are still drawn as an image — they are described in text (the data summary, the axis names and the list of events) and are also reachable through the month slider, the series checkboxes and the linked table
    • screen readers in browse mode (NVDA/JAWS) may need focus mode before arrow keys reach the chart itself, and the month slider, a standard form control, offers another way to move through the months
    • Excel's accessibility checker has not been run on the XLSX files
    • The whole site requires JavaScript — loading the data, moving between pages and choosing the language — and without it a notice in Hebrew and English says so.
    • The notice about part of the site not loading detects only a site script file that did not load at all or stopped before registering itself; a file that loads and fails later, while running, is not detected by it. The visit counter script is not checked by it, and the chart library has its own message in the chart section
    • The descriptions of the screenshots in the guides and the accessible names of components cannot mark the language of part of their text, so English interface labels inside them are not marked as English; in the rest of the interface text, which is translated as whole units — for example "Export to PDF" in the tools, the navigation, headings and field labels — English terms are not marked either

    How accessibility was tested

    The site's accessibility was tested automatically and manually:

    • An axe-core scan across every page in Hebrew and English, including interface states such as errors, calculation results, an open chart and an open guide
    • Manual testing in a Chromium-based browser: keyboard-only navigation across every route and tool on the site, including the chart
    • A 320-pixel-wide view (and 305 pixels, a narrow window with a classic scrollbar) with no horizontal page scroll and no overlapping components, text enlarged to 200% and increased text spacing
    • An overflow check on every page of the site, in Hebrew and English, at window widths from 305 to 1100 pixels, at normal text size, at text enlarged to 200% and at text enlarged to 200% together with increased text spacing: no horizontal page scroll
    • Click-target size, the contrast of text and of interface components, and focus that is not hidden under the sticky header
    • A scan of the English version for text left in Hebrew, in dozens of interface states (results, errors, tables and guides), for the options of the controls in the tools (long lists such as years were sampled), including switching the language and back, and in the export files (XLSX workbooks and the print sheet) in both languages
    • A check of Chrome's accessibility tree for unnamed controls and controls sharing a name in the same context, and a scan of component names on every route and state (in both languages, including the open language menu and the copy confirmation) for symbols that have crept into a name
    • A simulation of forced colors mode to find components with no border, with screenshots to confirm
    • A real keyboard walk (Tab) across every route of the site at 1100 and 375 pixels wide, to check that the focus indicator is not cut off at the edge of a scroll area, and also at text enlarged to 200%, to check that focus is not hidden under the sticky header
    • Blocking the data files from loading to check the notice about a loading failure on every route
    • Blocking each of the site's script files from loading in turn, to check the notice about part of the site not loading, and a real keyboard walk to the "Page not found" view and through the 404 error page

    No axe-core scan has been run since 15 October 2026; the changes made before the travel report, including the site map, were checked with the keyboard, for overflow and with the project's tests. The travel report was checked with calculation, translation, file-structure and focus tests against a mock interface; it has not yet been checked with a real screen reader, a keyboard walk-through in a browser or an accessibility scan.

    The later changes to the rental-agreement tool (manual month entry, the agreement-details table and the current-figures table) were checked with the project's tests against a mock interface and with an automated Chromium run (typing into the fields and an overflow check at widths of 320 and 1280 pixels); they have not been checked with a real screen reader, a manual keyboard walk-through or an accessibility scan.

    The changes in version 2.0.4 to the travel report (the allowed cap beside the expense fields, the first-class note button, the single lodging row over 90 nights and the children’s education group) were checked with the project's tests against a mock interface; they have not been checked with a real screen reader, a manual keyboard walk-through or an accessibility scan.

    The export files were checked too: a PDF produced in Chrome was inspected for tagging, language and title, and XLSX files were opened with a spreadsheet library to check the document properties, the frozen header, the filter and the absence of merged cells.

    The testing did not include a screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA, JAWS or TalkBack), browsers other than Chromium, real mobile devices, or testing with people with disabilities, and it is not a professional accessibility audit.

    This list of tests is partial: every round of testing added kinds of checks that found defects not found before, so it should not be read as confirmation that every defect has been found. Some of these kinds are now enforced by automated tests that fail when a new defect of the same kind appears.
